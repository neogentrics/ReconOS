The Calculator's Graph tab draws up to three curves at once, each in its
own colour, with a matching square beside the field it came from.

y of x is the ordinary kind: type an expression and it draws y against x.
Polar asks a different question -- the expression is the distance from the
origin, and the angle sweeps round. sin(3*t) is a three-petal rose,
1+cos(t) is a heart, and t/3 is a spiral.

The variable can be written x or t under either mode -- there is only one,
so both mean the same thing. Multiplication has to be written out: 3*t, not
3t.

Switching modes keeps what you typed and resets the view, because the two need
very different scales. It is worth doing once with something in the fields: the
same three lines look completely different under the other question.

Dragging moves the plane, the wheel zooms about the pointer, Reset
goes back to the starting view for whichever mode you are in, and Save
writes the picture into your Pictures folder under the date and time.

A curve that has no value somewhere is broken rather than joined across the
gap, so 1/x is drawn without the vertical line down the y axis that most
graphers draw and call part of the function.
